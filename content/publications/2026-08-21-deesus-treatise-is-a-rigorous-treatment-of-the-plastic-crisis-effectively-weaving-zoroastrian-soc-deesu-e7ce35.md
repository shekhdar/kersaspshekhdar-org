DEESU’s treatise is a rigorous treatment of the plastic crisis, effectively weaving Zoroastrian, Socratic, Buddhist, Aristotelian, and Nietzschean concepts into a coherent ethical critique. The four-pillar proposal is ambitious and largely well-founded.

The production cap modelled on the Montreal Protocol is the right strategic choice; however, its success depends on making Essential-Use Exemptions narrowly defined and time-limited, with transparent, independently audited oversight. The design mandate separating durable techno-polymers from true biodegradables is conceptually elegant, though it will require a robust standard for ambient biodegradability and global testing protocols to prevent greenwashing. The Cleanup Corps and “Bottle-to-Brick” initiative offer a just transition but need pilot-scale demonstration and life-cycle assessment to verify net environmental benefit.

The cultural revaluation section is persuasive, particularly the emphasis on combating “advanced recycling” sophistry. Overall, the treatise meets the mission of proposing well-founded, novel solutions, and it maintains a strong ethical foundation without falling into mere technocratic optimism.
