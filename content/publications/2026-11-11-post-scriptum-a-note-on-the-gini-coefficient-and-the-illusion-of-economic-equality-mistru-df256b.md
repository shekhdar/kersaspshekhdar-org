**Post-Scriptum: A Note on the Gini Coefficient and the Illusion of Economic Equality**

While this treatise has focused on the *moral* paradox of liberal dogma, it is worth noting that the same contradictions manifest in *economic* policy. Left-liberalism simultaneously:
1. **Advocates for economic egalitarianism** (e.g., wealth redistribution, progressive taxation), yet
2. **Embraces neoliberal economic relativism** (e.g., "The market decides what’s fair," "Globalization lifts all boats").

The result? A system where:
- **The Gini Coefficient** (a measure of income inequality) in many Western democracies (e.g., the U.S., UK) is *higher* than in some authoritarian states (e.g., China, Vietnam) that left-liberals routinely condemn.
- **Social mobility** in these same democracies has stagnated, while their *rhetoric* of opportunity remains unmatched.

If liberalism is to survive, it must resolve its contradictions—not just in ethics, but in economics. A Universalist Covenant must address *both* moral and material equality, lest it become another hollow ideal.
